The Sun is a star at the center of our solar system. It is made mostly of hydrogen and helium, and its gravity keeps the planets in orbit.

Mercury is the closest planet to the Sun. It has a rocky surface, very little atmosphere, and large temperature changes between day and night.

Venus has a thick carbon dioxide atmosphere that traps heat. Its surface is hot enough to melt lead, making it the hottest planet in the solar system.

Earth is the only planet known to support life. Its moderate temperatures allow liquid water to remain on the surface, and its atmosphere contains oxygen.

Earth has one large natural satellite called the Moon. The Moon reflects sunlight, and its gravity helps cause ocean tides on Earth.

Mars looks red because iron oxide, or rust, covers much of its surface. It has tall volcanoes, deep valleys, and two small moons.

Between Mars and Jupiter lies the asteroid belt. It contains many rocky objects, including the dwarf planet Ceres, orbiting the Sun.

Jupiter is the largest planet in the solar system. It is a gas giant with a Great Red Spot, a huge storm that has lasted for centuries.

Saturn is famous for its bright rings. The rings are made mostly of pieces of ice, along with smaller amounts of rock and dust.

Uranus is an ice giant with a blue-green color caused by methane in its atmosphere. It rotates on its side compared with most other planets.

Neptune is the farthest major planet from the Sun. Its deep blue atmosphere has some of the strongest winds measured in the solar system.

Pluto is a dwarf planet in the Kuiper Belt beyond Neptune. It has a heart-shaped region on its surface and several small moons.
