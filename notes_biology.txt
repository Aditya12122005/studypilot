All living organisms are made of cells, the basic units of life. Some organisms consist of one cell, while plants and animals contain many specialized cells.

A cell membrane surrounds each cell and controls what enters and leaves the cell. The cytoplasm is a watery interior where many cell activities take place.

The nucleus stores DNA, the molecule that contains most of a cell's inherited instructions. DNA helps direct cell activities and can be copied when cells divide.

DNA has a double helix shape. Its bases pair in a specific way: adenine pairs with thymine, and cytosine pairs with guanine.

A gene is a section of DNA that contributes instructions for a particular product or trait. Different versions of a gene are called alleles, and they can contribute to variation among individuals.

Mitochondria release energy from glucose using oxygen during cellular respiration. Cells use this released energy to power many of their activities.

Enzymes are biological catalysts. Enzymes speed reactions by lowering activation energy and are not consumed by the reactions they help.

In photosynthesis, chloroplasts capture light energy. The process uses carbon dioxide and water to make glucose and oxygen.

Roots absorb water and minerals from soil, while stomata in leaves allow gases to move in and out. Leaves contain chloroplasts where photosynthesis can occur.

Xylem carries water and minerals upward, while phloem distributes sugars made in leaves to other parts of a plant. These vascular tissues connect roots, stems, and leaves.

During aerobic respiration, cells use glucose and oxygen to release energy, producing carbon dioxide and water. Respiration happens in many kinds of cells, not only in animals.

Arteries carry blood away from the heart, while veins return blood to the heart. Capillaries connect small arteries and veins and allow exchange with nearby tissues.

Red blood cells use hemoglobin to carry oxygen, while white blood cells attack pathogens. Platelets help blood clot after a blood vessel is damaged.

Vaccines train the immune system to recognize a pathogen, and memory cells support a faster response if the pathogen appears again. Different vaccines target different pathogens.

Ecosystems include living organisms and their physical environment. Decomposers break down dead material and return nutrients to the environment, where they can be reused.
